**Objective:** Prototype a national payment switch using open-source platforms to interconnect Sudanese banks and mobile money systems.  
**Relevance:** Addresses Sudan’s collapsed finance system. Japan excels in fintech (via SoftBank, NEC, central bank research) and can guide system design. The project has high diplomatic significance (economic recovery aid) and technical merit (network design). It ties to Japan’s work on digital public infrastructure.  
**Background:** A study recommends using Mojaloop to build Sudan’s interoperable financial network. India’s UPI shows huge gains from such a system. No unified Sudanese system exists; rebuilding it will revive cash aid and commerce.  
**Methods/Data:** Using the Mojaloop sandbox, simulate a Sudanese payment environment: define APIs for banks, telecom e-money, and remittance NGOs. Model transaction flows between agents (remitters, beneficiaries). Evaluate performance under network outages. Compare to cash-only baseline in terms of cost/time. Also map regulatory requirements (with input from Sudan’s central bank).  
**Expected Results:** A report and demonstration of a working prototype linking at least two “bank” endpoints and a mobile money operator. Metrics on transaction speed and resilience. Policy recommendations for pilot deployment.  
**Impact:** Directly helps restore Sudan’s economy by enabling fast, low-cost payments (especially for humanitarian cash transfers). Cement Japan’s role as a tech partner in economic reconstruction.  
**Timeline:**

- M1–3: Familiarize with Mojaloop, define system requirements (with partner central bank).
- M4–6: Develop prototype (test network, basic UI).
- M7–9: Simulate transactions and stress-test.
- M10–12: Analyze results and finalize thesis.

## Posture 
Exercise Routine to Improve Posture

Based on the video "Try these 3 habits to fix your posture," here is a simple and effective routine focusing on three key exercises that target grip strength, glutes, and deltoids to improve posture aesthetically and functionally.

---

### 1. Bar Hangs (Grip and Upper Body)
- **Purpose:** Strengthen grip, stretch lats, decompress spine, and activate upper back muscles.
- **How to do it:**
  - Find a pull-up bar.
  - **Passive hang:** Simply hang with relaxed shoulders to stretch lats and decompress spine.
  - **Active hang:** Hang while pulling shoulder blades back and activating upper back muscles.
- **Duration:**
  - Beginners: Aim for 15-30 seconds.
  - Intermediate: Aim for 1 minute.
  - Advanced: Aim for 2 minutes.
- **Frequency:** Ideally every day; if not possible, at least 3 times a week.
- **Tip:** Beginners can keep feet lightly touching the ground for support.

### 2. Glute Bridges (Glutes)
- **Purpose:** Strengthen glutes to support standing tall and improve posture.
- **How to do it:**
  - Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
  - Lift hips towards the ceiling, squeezing glutes at the top.
  - Hold the position for 5-10 seconds (glute bridge hold).
- **Repetitions:** Start with 15 reps.
- **Frequency:** Daily, preferably in the morning to activate glutes early.
- **Progression:** If you have gym access, progress to hip thrusts with weights for added strength.

---

### 3. Face Pulls (Deltoids and Upper Back)
- **Purpose:** Strengthen rhomboids and upper back to retract shoulder blades and improve thoracic spine posture.
- **How to do it:**
  - Use a resistance band or cable machine with a rope attachment.
  - Pull the band or cable towards your face, focusing on squeezing shoulder blades together.
- **Repetitions:** Perform 2-3 sets of 10-15 reps.
- **Frequency:** Include in your routine 2-3 times per week.

---

### Additional Tips
- **Movement Variety:** Change your sitting or standing posture every 15-20 minutes to avoid stiffness.
- **Focus on Comfort:** There is no perfect posture; prioritize what feels comfortable and natural.
- **Consistency:** Regular practice of these exercises will gradually improve posture aesthetics and reduce discomfort.

---

By following this routine consistently, you can strengthen key muscle groups that support good posture and reduce the risk of back pain and discomfort.

If you want to track progress, consider timing your bar hangs and increasing duration gradually for motivation.

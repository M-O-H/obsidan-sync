```
You are an expert in learning science and skill acquisition. 
I want to learn [TOPIC/SKILL]. My goal is to reach functional 
competence — meaning I can produce real outputs — as efficiently 
as possible.

Execute the following material selection protocol in order.

---

PHASE 1 — DEFINE THE TARGET OUTPUT

Define in one to two sentences what a functional (not expert) 
practitioner in [TOPIC/SKILL] actually produces day-to-day. 
Focus on output, not knowledge. If the target output is unclear, 
state what I need to find out first before selecting any materials.

---

PHASE 2 — GENERATE A CANDIDATE LIST

Generate a list of five to eight high-quality learning resources 
for [TOPIC/SKILL]. Selection criteria:

- Prioritize resources recommended by active practitioners, 
  not general popularity rankings.
- Include at least one long-form written resource (book or 
  documentation), one project-based or applied course, and one 
  practitioner blog, case study, or annotated reference.
- Briefly state why each resource made the list — specifically 
  what signals indicate it is practitioner-oriented.

Do not evaluate or rank them yet.

---

PHASE 3 — APPLY THE EVALUATION FILTER

For each resource from Phase 2, evaluate it against these 
four criteria and assign a score of High, Medium, or Low:

1. AUTHOR CREDIBILITY — Is the author primarily a practitioner 
   or primarily an educator? Practitioners reference personal 
   failures, specific tradeoffs, and applied context. Educators 
   tend toward comprehensive coverage and structured progression. 
   Practitioner resources rank higher.

2. TIME TO ACTIVE PRACTICE — At what point in the resource does 
   the learner first produce something? If active practice begins 
   beyond the first 30% of the material, score this criterion low.

3. MIDDLE-SECTION DEPTH — Does the resource progress into nuanced 
   application and real-world tradeoffs, or does it remain at a 
   foundational level throughout? Assess based on what the 
   middle section covers, not the introduction.

4. ACTIONABILITY — After completing this resource, can the 
   learner name one specific thing they would do differently? 
   If the answer requires finishing the entire resource to know, 
   score this criterion low.

Present the evaluation as a table with each resource as a row 
and each criterion as a column, with a final overall score.

---

PHASE 4 — SELECT AND SEQUENCE

Based on Phase 3 scores, recommend:

- One PRIMARY resource: the highest-scored resource most directly 
  oriented toward the target output defined in Phase 1.
- One REFERENCE resource: for resolving specific gaps that appear 
  during practice.
- One DEPTH resource: for understanding why something works when 
  the primary resource does not explain it.

For each selection, state explicitly why it was chosen over the 
alternatives. Then provide a recommended sequence: what to engage 
with first, when to consult the reference, and when the depth 
resource becomes relevant.

---

PHASE 5 — VALIDATION CRITERIA

State the three questions I should answer after each of my 
first two sessions with the primary resource:

1. Did I produce something, even if small?
2. Did I encounter a difficulty that revealed a gap I did 
   not previously know I had?
3. Do I understand not just what to do, but why it works?

If any answer is no after two sessions, identify which resource 
from the candidate list should be promoted to primary and why.

---

FORMAT REQUIREMENTS

Present the output in five clearly labeled sections corresponding 
to each phase. Be specific — avoid generic recommendations. 
Every claim about a resource's quality must be tied to a concrete 
characteristic of that resource, not its general reputation
```

```
You are an expert learning strategist.
Your task is to select high-quality learning materials for the topic: [TOPIC].
Follow this strict evaluation process:
1. Goal Identification
* Determine if the goal is: understanding, practical application, or both.
1. Candidate Selection
* Choose only 2–3 materials from trusted sources (experts, official docs, well-known authors).
1. Quality Evaluation Criteria Evaluate each material based on:
* Accuracy (aligned with trusted/official sources)
* Clarity (explains WHY, not just WHAT)
* Structure (logical progression from basics to advanced)
* Signal Density (focus on essential concepts, no fluff)
* Transferability (helps apply knowledge in new situations)
1. Testing Simulation
* Simulate a 10–20 minute learning session from each material
* Judge whether the concept becomes clear and explainable
1. Selection Decision
* Pick ONE primary material (best overall quality)
* Optionally select ONE secondary material (for clarification only)
* Ensure at least one source supports practical application
```
